# 그림자가 모르는 것

**What the Shadows Do Not Know** · Original sculpture and rendered still · 2026

한쪽에서는 집, 다른 쪽에서는 나무. 가운데에는 비어 있는 몸 하나.

## Work

A single terracotta-colored body stands between two pale walls. Light traveling in one horizontal direction casts a house; light traveling in the perpendicular direction casts a tree. The visible body is hollow. Neither familiar silhouette describes that opening.

The image is intentionally quiet: no labels, explanatory arrows, surrounding objects, or narrative characters. Its geometry carries the disagreement between a recognizable outline and an unfamiliar interior.

## Development

The first body was the intersection of two extruded profiles. It produced the required shadows, but its bulky center did little beyond solving the geometry. Removing an offset interior kept both profiles while making an open shell. An accidental projecting shelf made the cavity look furnished. That shelf was recessed into the shell, leaving one continuous empty space.

A second camera position made the house too narrow and crowded the tree. The final view retains more space between the three forms. Early renders and rejected variants remain locally preserved in the ignored `studies/` directory.

## Files

- `One-Body-Two-Shadows.png`: final 1600 × 1200 image.
- `One-Body-Two-Shadows.blend`: editable scene.
- `build.py`: original profiles, Boolean construction, room, lights and camera.
- `render.py`: conventional full-frame rendering.
- `render_region.py`, `assemble_regions.py`: bounded-memory regional rendering and lossless pixel assembly used for the final image.
- `check_mesh_and_masks.py`: evaluated geometry checks and orthographic silhouette renders.
- `compare_profiles.py`: independent equation-based comparison against the intended profiles.
- `geometry-qa.json`, `readback-qa.json`, `projection-qa.json`: generated numerical checks.
- `description.txt`: short Korean caption.

## Reproduce

Blender 4.3.2, CPU rendering. Python comparison requires NumPy, SciPy and Pillow. No downloaded meshes, textures or fonts are used.

```sh
blender -b -t 6 --python-exit-code 1 --python build.py -- final hollow
blender -b -t 6 --python-exit-code 1 --python check_mesh_and_masks.py
python3 compare_profiles.py
for k in $(seq 0 11); do
  blender -b -t 6 --python-exit-code 1 --python render_region.py -- "$k"
done
python3 assemble_regions.py
python3 check_region_overlap.py
python3 verify_delivery.py
```

The final render uses Cycles, up to 512 samples and adaptive sampling, without a denoiser. The checker does not save its diagnostic changes over the beauty scene. Full-frame saves were interrupted, so the delivered image uses twelve independently rendered regions with eight-pixel margins. Only the exact interior pixels are assembled; no resizing, retouching or painted shadows are applied. All 17 adjacent overlaps have mean absolute RGB differences below 0.439 on the 0–255 scale, with 99th-percentile differences no greater than 2. Final boundaries are also inspected visually.

## Verification and boundaries

The source body is one connected, closed mesh. The evaluated beveled mesh has no non-manifold edges and positive signed volume. A uniform-density centroid lies over the base extents. That is a limited geometric check, not a load, material, ceramic firing, fabrication or tipping-resistance assessment.

Orthographic silhouettes are rendered from the saved geometry and compared with separately sampled house and tree equations at 512 × 512. Their overlap exceeds 99.6%; small differences come from polygon discretization, bevels and raster boundaries. The house has one enclosed window hole, while the tree has none. The arch doorway opens onto the outer boundary.

These numbers assess object silhouettes, not pixel registration of the perspective appearance of the wall shadows. The two wall shadows are generated by the scene lights and reviewed visually. No shadow silhouettes are painted onto the receiving walls.
