Create a complete original fine-art image titled conceptually "A Place Left Unswept", without any visible lettering. Landscape 3:2.
We look almost straight down at a pale slate-gray concrete courtyard after light autumn rain. Most of the frame is an expansive quiet surface, with broad curved sweep marks and a few water-darkened patches. Slightly left of center and below the midpoint remains one small irregular island of tawny gold and rust-red fallen leaves. At its near edge, visibly and clearly a single small snail with a warm umber spiral shell extends its dark soft body and two delicate upper feelers onto the clean concrete, moving toward the lower left. The snail is small in the image but not lost: its shell is about one tenth the width of the leaf island. The leaves are ordinary ginkgo fans and a few narrow brown leaves, partly overlapping, never a perfect circle.
From the upper-right edge, the head of a traditional straw broom enters diagonally. Its long, gently splayed ochre bristles end a short distance before the leaf island, visibly stopped, leaving the snail room. Only a short section of its plain wooden handle appears cropped at the top-right edge. No hand, no person, no other animals. No literal drawn boundary around the island. The sweep direction is suggested by subtle thin curved traces on the damp ground, passing around rather than through the small leaf patch.
Style: a tactile original color lithograph combined with dry gouache, confident flat shapes, soft broken ink edges, sparse scratched marks, understated paper grain. Not photorealistic, not a digital 3D render, not vector clip art. The visual subject is the tension between the great swept empty gray space and the small warm irregular patch allowed to remain, with the tiny moving creature as a discovery. Restrained palette of warm off-white concrete, cool blue-gray dampness, burnt sienna, faded mustard straw, and one dark umber snail. Shape and negative space matter more than decorative texture. Asymmetric composition; the broom is secondary and cropped, the leaf island sits low-left; no sentimental effects, no sparkles, no captions, no border, no watermark. Give the whole surface the imperfect variation of a print while retaining distinct edges at the snail, leaves, and stopped bristle tips.
