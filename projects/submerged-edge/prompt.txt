Use case: stylized-concept. Make a finished original relief-print-style artwork, not a mockup of a framed print. Subject: an intimate cropped view of one massive irregular coastal rock at the edge of a still shallow tidal pool. Explore the difference between dry stone, wet stone, and water using the character of marks rather than different colors. A single deep blue-black ink on warm ivory paper, essentially two-tone, with no smooth grayscale shading and no photographic gradients. The dry upper rock is built from coarse angular gouges, sparse broken chips and heavy planes. Its wet lower edge becomes a dense dark continuous mass. The pool is a contrasting broad quiet dark shape with a few long narrow ivory reflections and interrupted ripples. The waterline wraps convincingly around the rock but is not a ruler-straight horizon. Cropped very close: the rock enters from the left and top edges and occupies roughly two thirds of the image; the quiet open pool spreads toward the lower right. No whole-rock silhouette floating on a blank page, no scenic horizon, no sunset, no mountains, no trees, no animals, no people. Make the meeting of the rough carved stone and the calm open water the real subject. Flat graphic interlocking areas, forceful asymmetry, large unworked spaces balanced against concentrated detail, imperfect cut edges, restrained print texture rather than all-over noise. It should feel specific, austere, and tactile, not like a decorative Japanese-wave imitation, a nature logo, a coloring page, or a generic landscape poster. Landscape 3:2, full bleed, no paper margin, no signature, no title, no lettering, no frame. Keep the design legible at a distance while rewarding close inspection of the rock-water contact.
